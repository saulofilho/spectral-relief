Spectral Relief

Sound becomes a mountain range. Each moment of the spectrum is a ridge; time pushes the ridges back, where they fade and vanish into the mist. The ink comes from the print shop: halftone, stipple or line screen, never a smooth gradient.

Moodboard

The mountain as a printed chart

Two found references and two studies printed by the engine itself make the same move: a landscape you read like data. None of them uses color, and none uses a continuous outline. The shape comes from ink density, and the edge is always jagged by the dot itself.

Colour · third mode · after TORUS

Normal: the colour is the direction the rock faces

Borrowed from the TORUS experiment, which paints a spinning torus with p5's normalMaterial and never clears the canvas. Here each slope is coloured by where it points: faces turned right go red, crests looking up go green, faces looking at you go blue, on black. With Trails on, the old frame fades instead of being erased, so the past ridges leave the same ribbons the torus left.

Firmware · FM-1

70% of the proof, 3 px minimum
Layers
55% of the proof
Spectrum
1024-point integer FFT on the firmware's audio

The engine is ported to C in fm1/: a 1024-point integer FFT, no libm, no framebuffer. It fills the screen in strips, the way the FM-1 firmwares already draw, and computes the print screens per pixel instead of storing a threshold map. All three colour modes and screens are in; Normal's trails need a full 240 × 240 framebuffer, so they are off by default.

Runs under 1 ms a frame on a laptop. Not yet run on the device: the next step is a firmware page, measuring FFT cost per block and the frame rate left after audio.

Max for Live · Ableton Live

Device strip, 169 px tall

Drawing

Theme
Light ink on Live's graphite
Spectrum
pfft~ 2048, Hann, into a buffer~

It sits in the chain right after the instrument and draws what it plays. Sebastian Mitchell's Wavetable redesign study is a good yardstick: each unison voice became a readable line. Here, each harmonic becomes a peak.

The device is in m4l/: pfft~ writes one amplitude per bin into a buffer~, and a v8ui script with the same engine draws it, with Ink, Colour, Depth, Height, Rough, Screen, Trails and Freeze as Live parameters.

Built and tested against a mock of Max's JavaScript API; not yet opened in Live.

Principles

Rules for the next proofs

Density, not strokes

Every shape comes from the amount of ink. No vector lines, no smooth gradients, no glow.

The past fades

Back layers lose contrast and gain mist. The front is dark, rough and sharp.

Annotate like an engineer

Blue monospaced labels with real units: Hz, note, Q. The mountain is a chart you can read.

Big dots on small screens

On synth displays, the screen size drops to 3–4 px and line mode wins: it reads well in 1 bit.

Jagged is honest

The serrated edge comes from the grid and the detune. Don't smooth it.

Silence draws too

Without sound, a plain and dust remain. The screen is never empty.
