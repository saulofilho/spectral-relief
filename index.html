The mountain as a printed chart

All four references make the same move: a landscape you read like data. None of them uses color, and none uses a continuous outline. The shape comes from ink density, and the edge is always jagged by the dot itself.

Inks

AM halftone · 45°

The dot grows. Reads best in large masses and in the slow movement of a pad.

FM stipple

The dot multiplies. Better for fast attacks and transients.

Line screen

The line thickens. Recalls engraving and the pixels of the fourth reference.

Each layer fades at its own base. That is what separates the ridges.

Principles

Rules for the next proofs

Density, not strokes

Every shape comes from the amount of ink. No vector lines, no smooth gradients, no glow.

The past fades

Back layers lose contrast and gain mist. The front is dark, rough and sharp.

Annotate like an engineer

Blue monospaced labels with real units: Hz, note, Q. The mountain is a chart you can read.

Big dots on small screens

On synth displays, the screen size drops to 3–4 px and line mode wins: it reads well in 1 bit.

Jagged is honest

The serrated edge comes from the grid and the detune. Don't smooth it.

Silence draws too

Without sound, a plain and dust remain. The screen is never empty.
